About

The Echo Foundry Institute

Good things are already happening in classrooms across our school. But they are isolated, without a shared language or coordinating purpose. This proposal brings them together.

The Gap

Creative projects, technology integration, and student-led learning exist across our school. But when innovation is left to individual teachers, it lives and dies with those individuals and never becomes a culture.

The Opportunity

Christian schools are navigating AI, shifting workforce expectations, and students who need more than content — they need formation. We have a moment to lead, not by chasing trends, but by anchoring innovation to a clear theological identity.

What We’re Proposing

  • Student innovation programming (PK–12)
  • Teacher development & cohort leadership
  • AI / technology literacy as a formation challenge
  • Signature showcases & real-world application
  • A shared mission language across all divisions

Mission, Vision & Stability

Mission

The Echo Foundry Institute exists to develop and form teachers and students as image-bearers of God who cultivate wisdom, creativity, and stewardship through hands-on innovation, collaborative learning, and purposeful service that reflects Christ in the world.

Vision

A generation of teachers and students formed as ambassadors for Christ who think creatively, communicate clearly, lead wisely, and serve faithfully — equipped through integrated learning experiences to design solutions, express ideas, and address real-world challenges in ways that reflect God’s wisdom, creativity, and redemptive purpose in the world.

Stability Statement

While tools, methods, and platforms will evolve, the purpose of education — to form students as ambassadors for Christ who create, communicate, lead, and serve with wisdom and integrity — remains unchanged. Emerging technologies are embraced as instruments for learning and expression, not as drivers of identity or purpose.

A name is the first thing people encounter before they walk through a door, attend an orientation, or read a brochure. For an institute within a Christian K–12 school, a name carries extraordinary weight when it is theology made visible.

ECHO
Equipping · Cultivating · Honoring · Overcoming

We are made to echo God’s image back into the world. Students don’t originate — they reflect, amplify, and carry forward what they’ve received. That’s formation. Echo is both an acronym and a rich theological word: image-bearers who reverberate God’s wisdom and creativity into every domain of life.

FOUNDRY
Where raw material is refined into something lasting

A foundry signals making and craft, not just consuming. Transformation, not just information. Tools being forged for real work in the world. Unlike terms like makerspace or innovation hub, Foundry has historical and theological weight. It won’t age.

Together, Echo Foundry says:

We form students who reflect God’s image into the world through purposeful making.

This is not a new department. It is a designation layer on top of existing staff — a coordinating framework, not a new budget line.

Leadership roles are assigned to current faculty. Programs build on what is already working. The goal is coherence, not addition.

The Echo Foundry Framework Proposal

To equip educators and students to thoughtfully, ethically, and creatively integrate artificial intelligence into teaching and learning through collaboration, innovation, and Christ-centered discernment.

Areas of Exploration

  • Piloting approved AI platforms and instructional workflows
  • Exploring ways AI can support planning, differentiation, creativity, and productivity
  • Identifying appropriate student use cases and boundaries
  • Discussing challenges related to academic integrity and critical thinking
  • Sharing practical classroom examples and implementation reflections
  • Collaborating on best practices for responsible AI integration
  • Helping shape future professional learning opportunities and schoolwide guidance

Quarterly Collaboration Meetings

  • Sharing implementation experiences
  • Evaluating successes and challenges
  • Discussing emerging tools and trends
  • Reflecting on ethical considerations and instructional impact
  • Collaborating on recommendations for future implementation

Guiding Principles

Technology should serve learning, not replace it.

  • Creativity and innovation
  • Critical thinking and discernment
  • Responsible leadership
  • Meaningful student engagement
  • Christ-centered learning and formation

Vision

A school community where emerging technologies enhance creativity, critical thinking, communication, and meaningful learning while students and educators engage with AI wisely, responsibly, and as ambassadors for Christ.

Structure & People

The Team

These Institute roles are designed as leadership designations, not new positions, assigned to existing faculty and staff to support and advance the mission across the school community.

Role Primary Focus Reports To
Director of Echo Foundry Institute K–12 Vision & Strategic Leadership. Oversees mission, long-term growth, partnerships, framework development, school integration, and alignment across all campuses and divisions. Associate Head of School
Director of Faculty Innovation & Professional Learning K–12 Teacher Development & Implementation. Leads professional development, faculty coaching, innovation cohorts, and collaborative teacher learning across divisions. Associate Head of School
Campus Director of Innovation & Learning Design (ES) Designs and implements PK–5 innovation experiences focused on wonder, creativity, foundational leadership, interdisciplinary learning, and signature showcases. Director of Faculty Innovation
Campus Director of Innovation & Learning Design (MS) Develops middle school pathways emphasizing collaboration, identity formation, exploration of specializations, innovation projects, and applied problem-solving. Director of Faculty Innovation
Campus Director of Innovation & Learning Design (HS) Oversees advanced pathways, entrepreneurship, media, STEM specialization, internships, portfolio development, leadership experiences, and real-world application. Director of Faculty Innovation
Foundry Innovation Lead (ES / MS / HS) Serves as innovation leader and campus implementation partner, helping pilot ideas, support faculty, and test emerging tools. Campus Director of Innovation & Learning Design

Teacher-leaders who help advance a culture of creativity, innovation, and Christ-centered learning across their division through collaboration, experimentation, and professional leadership.

Pilot & Evaluate

Test emerging instructional strategies, tools, and technologies to enhance student learning and assess real-world instructional impact.

Collaborate & Design

Work with Institute leadership to design, refine, and expand innovative programming across campus divisions.

Support Colleagues

Serve as a resource and point of contact for faculty, providing guidance and support in adopting new practices and technologies.

Share & Improve

Share insights, outcomes, and recommendations with fellow cohort members to inform continuous improvement across the school.

Foster Culture

Cultivate a culture of creativity, collaboration, and future-focused learning among colleagues and within the campus community.

Signature Showcases

Contribute to the development and execution of signature learning experiences and campus showcases that highlight student formation.
